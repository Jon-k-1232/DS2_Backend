# DS2 industry-standard assessment: accounts receivable and time & billing

Prepared for Jon Kimmel • 25 September 2026 • Read-only assessment

## 1. Executive summary

**Jon, DS2 has a credible foundation, but I would not yet call the complete system industry standard for a small CPA/tax firm.** The strongest parts are statement finalization, preservation of issued records, retainer movements, and the new audit evidence. The remaining weaknesses include accounting information that can mislead an owner, missing financial controls, and an actual failure to separate development from outbound services. This is more consequential than a list of convenience features. Sources: [invoice contract:9](../invoicing/invoices.md#L9), [audit integrity:55](../platform/audit-ledger.md#L55), [AR calculation:80](../../src/endpoints/accountsReceivable/accounts-receivable-service.js#L80), [development scheduler:179](../../src/app.js#L179).

**Keep your basic rules.** Balance-forward statements and “finalize = sent and locked” are workable business policies. The seven decisions—locked issued records, controlled bounced-check corrections, retainer refunds/adjustments, duplicate review, optional credit statements, six-minute rounding, and immutable audit records—should remain the foundation. The audit design exceeds my minimum expectation for a small firm's custom billing application, although its production permissions and archival protection remain **unverified**. Sources: [owner decisions:9](../decisions/2026-09-24-owner-decisions.md#L9), [seven-decision register:319](../../scripts/review-2026-09/FINAL_REPORT.md#L319), [audit limitations:85](../platform/audit-ledger.md#L85).

The most important accounting finding is that **DS2's aging buckets age the newest statement, allowing carried debt to appear young again**. Some “billed,” “realization,” and margin metrics also use work entered rather than issued billings or collections. These should be corrected before relying on the dashboard to manage collections or pricing. Sources: [AR buckets:113](../../src/endpoints/accountsReceivable/accounts-receivable-service.js#L113), [analytics:60](../../src/endpoints/analytics/analytics-service.js#L60).

**Top 10 additions, ranked by importance:**

1. **Safe operating boundary:** isolate development; verify security, restricted credentials, backups and recovery.
2. **True receivable aging:** retain original obligations, due dates and payment allocations beneath balance-forward statements.
3. **Legal-entity accounting:** distinguish the tax/accounting and advisory businesses throughout billing and cash.
4. **Cash and GL reconciliation:** receipt headers, deposits, retainer liabilities and controlled accounting exports.
5. **Complete correcting documents:** credit/debit memos, controlled void/rebill, and refunds beyond retainer refunds.
6. **Period close:** locked accounting periods, reproducible historical balances and reviewed legacy corrections.
7. **Approval controls:** separate entry, approval, refund, write-off and rate-override authority.
8. **Enforceable billing agreements:** effective-dated rates and actual fixed/recurring/progress billing rules where used.
9. **Trustworthy KPIs:** distinguish WIP, billings, revenue and cash; preserve historical cost and rate assumptions.
10. **Collections and client payment workflow:** assigned follow-up, disputes, promises to pay, secure payment links and engagement integration.

The first nine address the gaps substantiated below; the tenth is mainly standard product polish. This ranking is not a reason to postpone security while doing accounting work. **I would strengthen DS2 in phases, rather than replace its working ledger rules or attempt to reproduce every competing product.** Local regression results are documented, but deployment, historical data repair and firm-wide compliance are **unverified** in this assessment. ### 3.1 Accounting correctness: AR age must survive statement rollover

**This is the highest-priority financial-reporting change.** DS2 selects the newest parent statement date, takes the latest child balance for each relevant chain, sums that balance, and puts the entire amount into a bucket based on that statement date. Carrying a debt into a new statement therefore changes its apparent age. The separate `oldest_open_charge_date` walks billed positive charges under an estimated FIFO assumption; it is not a record of how particular payments, credits and reversals were applied. [accounts-receivable-service.js:80](../../src/endpoints/accountsReceivable/accounts-receivable-service.js#L80), [accounts-receivable-service.js:167](../../src/endpoints/accountsReceivable/accounts-receivable-service.js#L167).

**Keep the balance-forward presentation. Add open obligations underneath it.** Store the original billing and due dates, original amount, residual amount, entity and applications. Default to oldest due debt unless a documented client instruction or authorized override specifies otherwise. Apply credit memos to their related obligations; a returned payment must reopen the obligations it paid and restore their original age. Do not use the service date as the invoice due date: it measures WIP age before billing, a different question. Practice CS's application reporting illustrates why allocation history is a normal AR capability. [Practice CS AR applications](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/help/practice-cs/reporting/accounts-receivable-applications-report).

Synthetic acceptance example: an original $400 obligation is over 90 days overdue; today's $100 fee is not yet due; a $150 payment is applied oldest first. The new statement may show **$350 total**, but correct aging remains **$250 over 90 days plus $100 not yet due**. Sending that statement must not turn all $350 into recent debt. Reversing the $150 must restore the old bucket. This is a proposed acceptance case, not client data or a test run.

Also distinguish gross debtor AR, client credit balances and unused retainers. DS2 deliberately includes signed billed credit balances in AR while keeping unbilled work and unused retainers separate. Preserve those distinctions, but do not let unrelated clients' credit balances obscure the gross receivables figure used for collection management or the GL presentation selected by the accountant. [Owner decisions:41](../decisions/2026-09-24-owner-decisions.md#L41), [scenario balances:11](../scenarios/README.md#L11).

For old records, reconcile allocations to available evidence. Where original aging cannot be reconstructed reliably, label an opening-balance age as unknown and have the accountant approve the treatment. Do not manufacture precise historical allocations merely to fill a report.

### 3.2 Accounting correctness: cash receipts are not payment applications

DS2 correctly separates unused retainer availability from amount due, and it supports applying an overpayment partly to AR and partly to a prepayment. However, its payment table is not a complete cash-receipts journal: a hold-only receipt returns `payment: null`; a $150 receipt paying $100 of AR can store only $100 as the payment and $50 as a retainer; a retainer draw creates a payment application without new cash. The prepayment root creation also does not preserve the supplied effective receipt date in a dedicated receipt-date field. [payment-logic.js:398](../../src/endpoints/payments/payment-logic.js#L398), [payment-logic.js:447](../../src/endpoints/payments/payment-logic.js#L447), [payment-logic.js:497](../../src/endpoints/payments/payment-logic.js#L497), [payment-logic.js:523](../../src/endpoints/payments/payment-logic.js#L523).

**Do not export a sum of payment rows as cash received.** Add an immutable gross-receipt record with received date, posting date, payer, entity, method, original amount, reference and source. Its child applications explain how much paid AR, became held credit or was refunded. Retainer use is a transfer/application, not another cash receipt. Link refund and return events explicitly to their source receipts; note strings should not be the primary accounting relationship.

Build deposit batches and bank-clearing/processor-settlement status. Reconcile gross receipts to deposits, deductions, returns and undeposited cash; reconcile each client's held funds to the held-credit control account. For money legally held in a separate client-funds account, reconcile bank, control ledger and client subledgers. Clio is a useful model for that last discipline, **not a basis for automatically imposing lawyers' trust/IOLTA rules on a CPA practice**. The firm's contracts, jurisdiction and actual funds arrangement determine the applicable treatment. [Clio reconciliation](https://help.clio.com/hc/en-us/articles/21058568234267-Clio-Accounting-Operating-and-Trust-Reconciliation).

The “last payment” AR query also selects the latest payment row and displays its absolute amount without filtering receipt versus retainer application versus returned-payment reversal. It therefore does not establish “last cash received.” Rename it or calculate actual receipt activity from the proposed receipt register. [accounts-receivable-service.js:194](../../src/endpoints/accountsReceivable/accounts-receivable-service.js#L194).

### 3.3 Accounting correctness: two businesses need separate accounting identities

The owner-supplied scope is two businesses sharing `account_id = 1`; their live legal structure and bank arrangements were not investigated. The inspected invoices, transactions and payments have account/customer ownership but no explicit legal-business identifier. An audit `entity_id` identifies a changed record; it does not establish the issuing business. Shared access is therefore not, by itself, separate revenue and funds accounting. [Invoice schema:441](../../migrations/schema-snapshot-2026-09-22.sql#L441), [Transaction schema:758](../../migrations/schema-snapshot-2026-09-22.sql#L758), [Payment schema:581](../../migrations/schema-snapshot-2026-09-22.sql#L581), [audit coverage:11](../platform/audit-ledger.md#L11).

Add a legal-entity dimension to engagements/jobs, charges, obligations, invoices, receipts, held credits, adjustments and accounting mappings. Snapshot the issuer on issued documents. Define the recipient bank and legal owner of each credit. Report revenue, AR, cash and liabilities by entity, with a combined management view available above them. Do not silently use one entity's client credit to discharge another's bill; any allowed transfer needs explicit accounting and authorization.

This does **not** require forcing two application accounts or duplicating every client. One shared workspace can support entity subledgers. Jon and the accountant need to confirm legal names, business ownership, bank accounts, invoice numbering preferences, tax/accounting basis, and how shared staff costs are allocated. If advisory fees are computed and collected elsewhere, import reconciled fee events and their source evidence; do not build a portfolio-management platform solely to solve billing attribution.

### 3.4 Accounting correctness: invoice totals, revenue and GL exports

DS2 statements combine carried balance, eligible work and signed payment/adjustment activity. That is appropriate for the chosen presentation, but the full statement amount is not all newly earned revenue. The independent audit presentation already treats invoice totals as informational rather than adding a second debit. [totalInvoice.js:5](../../src/endpoints/invoice/createInvoice/invoiceCalculations/totalInvoice.js#L5), [audit presentation:63](../platform/audit-ledger.md#L63).

A QuickBooks or other GL bridge must export **new financial events**, not each statement's amount due. Otherwise carrying the same $400 into another statement could book it as revenue again. Prefer a modest, reviewed journal export before a complex bidirectional integration. Practice CS's period reconciliation is a useful benchmark for tying WIP, billing and AR movements together. [Practice CS period reconciliation](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/help/practice-cs/reporting/period-reconciliation-report).

Illustrative accrual mappings, to be approved for the firm's actual accounting and retainer policies:

| Event | Typical bookkeeping relationship | Control |
|---|---|---|
| New earned fee billed | Debit AR; credit appropriate fee revenue, with WIP/unbilled reclassification if previously recognized | Post new fee once; exclude carried balances |
| Cash pays AR | Debit cash/undeposited funds; credit AR | Match receipt, application and deposit |
| Advance held for future work | Debit cash; credit the accountant-selected advance/client-credit account | Preserve full receipt and client balance |
| Held credit applied | Debit advance/client-credit account; credit AR, or the selected equivalent treatment | No second cash receipt or duplicate revenue |
| Credit/write-off | Reduce AR against the correct concession, revenue adjustment, allowance or expense account | Reason and approved classification |
| Returned payment/refund | Reverse or settle the appropriate original cash/credit event | Preserve source link and bank evidence |

Cash-basis tax treatment may differ from an operational or accrual ledger; a book liability label alone does not determine when an advance is taxable. The accountant must approve mappings and avoid treating all write-offs as deductible bad debt. Each export needs entity, period, stable event IDs, balanced control totals, accepted/rejected status and replay protection. Choose one system of record for each object and define how corrections travel across the boundary. Ignition's QuickBooks guidance is a useful reminder that even commercial integrations have separate refund/manual-adjustment responsibilities. [Ignition and QuickBooks](https://support.ignitionapp.com/en/articles/600825-ignition-and-quickbooks).

### 3.5 Accounting correctness: correcting documents and closed periods

The current lock/reversal work is valuable: issued originals and membership are frozen, later financial events append snapshots, and a bounced-check exception supports a selected compensating reversal and a numbered revision without erasing the original. But the documented sent exception is specifically a bounced payment. An optional negative-balance **statement** is not a general **credit memo** documenting an approved fee reduction. Retainer refund/adjustment events likewise do not establish a general AR-credit refund workflow. [Invoices:38](../invoicing/invoices.md#L38), [Invoices:50](../invoicing/invoices.md#L50), [Invoices:69](../invoicing/invoices.md#L69), [Owner decisions:29](../decisions/2026-09-24-owner-decisions.md#L29).

Add dated credit/debit memos, approved bad-debt write-offs, and a controlled void/rebill path for wrong-client, wrong-entity or substantive fee errors. Keep original documents accessible and visibly linked to their replacements. A refund must identify whether it settles held funds or a billed credit and record its actual disbursement. Do not turn the bounced-check exception into a general unlock button.

Invoice locking is not period closing. The previous review explicitly leaves period policy, broader void/reissue and persisted billing runs as remaining work. Historical duplicate statements, possible double credits, sign exceptions, job links and stale WIP are listed for accountant decisions; their current resolution is **unverified**. New code does not automatically certify old balances. [FINAL_REPORT.md:45](../../scripts/review-2026-09/FINAL_REPORT.md#L45), [FINAL_REPORT.md:61](../../scripts/review-2026-09/FINAL_REPORT.md#L61).

Introduce service/receipt date, effective accounting date, posting period and actual creation time as distinct concepts. Close a period only after WIP, AR, receipts, held credit, deposits and GL totals reconcile. Permit late work to retain its original service date while posting an approved financial adjustment in an open period. Reopening requires an authorized reason and a retained before/after close package. Reconstructed audit history is useful evidence, but it should not be represented as complete contemporaneous history from before capture began. [Audit reconstruction:33](../platform/audit-ledger.md#L33).

### 3.6 Time capture, rate rules and WIP

**Already sound:** duration-based work uses a six-minute ceiling, and internal/non-work classification has server-side safeguards. This is a defensible chosen increment; no universal accounting-firm rule requires six minutes instead of another disclosed increment. Preserve actual minutes separately from billed tenths and explain rounding in engagement terms. Explicit quantity-only entries can still differ from duration-priced entries—for example, a supplied quarter-hour quantity is not automatically the same contract as 15 raw minutes rounded to 0.3 hours. The owner decision and current transaction validation document that distinction. [Transactions:138](../work/transactions.md#L138), [Transactions:148](../work/transactions.md#L148), [Transactions:239](../work/transactions.md#L239).

**Feature/control additions:** daily time completion, employee submission, manager approval, a record of postapproval changes, and clear billable/nonbillable/service categories. The inspected form offers decimal-hour entry; persistent running timers, restart recovery and mobile capture are **unverified**. Add a timer if it improves capture, but accurate daily entry and approval matter more than a stopwatch icon. A proposed timer should handle pause/resume, overlapping sessions, midnight, timezone boundaries and exact six-minute boundaries. [TimeOptions.js:11](../../../DS2_Frontend/src/Pages/Transactions/TransactionForms/AddTransaction/FormSubComponents/TimeOptions.js#L11), [TimeOptions.js:68](../../../DS2_Frontend/src/Pages/Transactions/TransactionForms/AddTransaction/FormSubComponents/TimeOptions.js#L68), [time ingestion:233](../platform/timesheets-and-ingestion.md#L233).

**Pricing concern:** ingestion uses the employee's current billing rate, while the customer/year agreement table is used by analytics. The inspected model does not establish effective-dated client/job/service/staff pricing precedence. Stored work prices do preserve the amount chosen at entry, but that does not ensure that the chosen rate matched the service date or engagement. [auto-ingest-orchestrator.js:591](../../src/endpoints/timesheets/auto-ingest-orchestrator.js#L591), [analytics-service.js:97](../../src/endpoints/analytics/analytics-service.js#L97), [Transaction schema:768](../../migrations/schema-snapshot-2026-09-22.sql#L768).

### 3.10 Reporting: useful views, but definitions must be corrected

DS2 already offers WIP aging, client-rate comparison, job budgets and a January–April tax-season capacity comparison by employee. Those are useful features. The immediate task is to make their meaning financially reliable rather than add another dashboard. [Analytics:190](../invoicing/analytics.md#L190), [Analytics:198](../invoicing/analytics.md#L198).

Four specific qualifications:

1. **“Billed” includes unbilled work.** Client-rate SQL sums billable transactions by service year without requiring an invoice link. It measures recorded charge value, not necessarily issued billings. [analytics-service.js:60](../../src/endpoints/analytics/analytics-service.js#L60).
2. **Current realization is not collection realization.** The calculation is `(recorded billable value − write-offs) / recorded billable value`; it does not measure cash collected against matched bills and does not preserve a distinct original-standard-value versus billing-adjustment bridge. [analytics-service.js:123](../../src/endpoints/analytics/analytics-service.js#L123).
3. **Historical margin uses today's employee cost rate.** Changing `users.cost_rate` changes calculated historical labor cost; missing cost is treated as zero. Non-time charges add value without labor cost in this calculation, so a fixed-fee job's nonbillable/included labor can be understated. This is not established client/job/staff profitability or “what the client paid.” [analytics-service.js:67](../../src/endpoints/analytics/analytics-service.js#L67), [analytics-service.js:130](../../src/endpoints/analytics/analytics-service.js#L130).
4. **The year-end packet is not a historical close package.** Its AR and WIP are current, the rates window is current, only time allocation is selected-year-specific, and its component reads do not share a snapshot. [Analytics packet:247](../invoicing/analytics.md#L247).

Rename misleading metrics promptly, then publish a metric dictionary with denominator, date basis, entity, adjustments and reconciliation source:

| Measure | Recommended definition/use |
|---|---|
| WIP | Unbilled work, separately showing holds, future work and pricing/completeness exceptions; age by service date |
| Billing realization | Net fee billed for matched work divided by its original standard value; distinguish write-up/down |
| Collection realization | Cash applied to a matched billing cohort divided by that cohort's agreed net billings; show refunds/returns separately |
| Utilization | Approved productive/billable hours divided by defined available capacity, with leave/internal work treated consistently |
| DSO | Average gross trade AR divided by credit fee billings per day for the selected period; exclude WIP and unrelated held credit; disclose seasonality |
| Collection effectiveness | CEI = 100 × (opening AR + period credit billings − closing total AR) / (opening AR + period credit billings − closing not-yet-due AR); identify noncash reductions separately and suppress a zero denominator. [NACM metrics, p. 4](https://nacm.org/pdfs/white-papers/Whitepaper-Metrics-apr23.pdf) |
| Profitability | Net earned/billed fee under the selected basis, less dated labor and direct costs; separate contribution margin from overhead-allocated profit |
| Tax-season view | Comparable service periods, weekly available capacity, WIP-to-bill lag, collection lag, missing time and overdue review queues |

APQC's DSO definition uses average gross AR divided by daily gross annual sales and excludes unbilled receivables. If the firm uses a shorter seasonal window or credit-fee denominator, label that variation so comparisons remain honest. Do not set an arbitrary “industry DSO target” without a relevant peer and client-mix benchmark. [APQC DSO](https://www.apqc.org/what-we-do/benchmarking/open-standards-benchmarking/measures/days-sales-outstanding).

Add entity/client/job/staff drill-down only after the financial and time denominators reconcile. A write-off can reduce closing AR and improve an unadjusted CEI without producing cash; display noncash reductions alongside it and never describe them as cash recovery. Fixed-fee work needs all attributable labor, including time deliberately not billed by the hour. Capacity should distinguish actual elapsed work from rounded billable units. Historical snapshots and effective-dated costs are dependencies, not optional cosmetic details.

## 4. “Above the standard” opportunities

These are realistic improvements after the P1 accounting and safety work, not prerequisites to buy every feature in a large suite.

1. **Owner exception inbox.** One weekly list of aged unbilled work, missed recurring fees, expired agreements, unmatched deposits, overdue promises, unusual write-offs and cross-entity exceptions. Explain the amount and required decision; avoid a dashboard full of unactionable scores.
2. **Transparent fee-renewal review.** Compare engagement scope, actual labor, accepted fee and collection experience; offer a proposed renewal price with its calculation and assumptions. Human approval remains mandatory. DS2's existing client-rate comparisons and budgets provide a starting point, after the metric corrections above. [Analytics:97](../../src/endpoints/analytics/analytics-service.js#L97), [Analytics guide:194](../invoicing/analytics.md#L194).
3. **Client-understandable account evidence.** Extend the existing readable Audit Record into a secure account explanation: original charge, credit/payment application, current due amount, held credit and the exact issued document. Preserve privacy and distinguish a client record from Full evidence. This builds on a genuine existing strength. [Audit presentation:61](../platform/audit-ledger.md#L61).
4. **A reconciled tax-season forecast.** Forecast billable completion, billing releases and probable cash timing from approved work, engagement schedules and actual receipt history. Show uncertainty and promised-payment dates; avoid promising cash based on AI confidence alone.
5. **A one-click close package.** Produce a fixed-period, hashed bundle of entity-level AR/WIP, receipt/deposit reconciliations, held funds, adjustments, approvals, GL export status and exceptions. Store approval and source cutoffs so a future accountant can reproduce the close.
6. **Early service and billing exceptions.** Flag missing time, a fixed-fee job approaching budget, completed work still on hold, or a recurring client with no charge. These deterministic checks are likely more valuable initially than another model-generated narrative.

Avoid turning this into a custom bank, document-signature provider or investment platform. The differentiator should be clear, explainable billing and a small amount of highly relevant firm automation.

**Priority P1; largest modeling dependency.** Design the entity dimension, original obligations, applications, gross receipts, held-credit movements and deposit register together. Reconcile current opening positions and accountant-approved historical corrections before importing legacy allocations. Produce an initial controlled GL export and a mapping workbook/policy, without requiring bidirectional synchronization.

**Decisions:** payment-allocation order and overrides; treatment of unknown legacy ages; allowed cross-entity transfers; account mappings; bank-clearing process; who signs the opening reconciliation.

**Exit evidence:** the synthetic aging example stays in its original buckets after rollover; cash receipts including holds/splits reconcile to deposits; retainer use creates no cash twice; each entity balances independently; replaying an export cannot duplicate a posting. Historical corrections retain the originally issued evidence.

### Phase 2 — Complete corrections, approvals and period close

**Priority P1, with P2 workflow improvements.** Implement credit/debit memos, general refunds and controlled void/rebill; capability permissions and threshold approvals; closed periods; historical as-of reports; and a persistent billing-run/recovery record. Complete the OCR file manifest and privacy corrections. The current narrow sent exception and missing file-level completeness are specific reasons for this phase. **Decisions:** prebill concession versus postbill bad-debt categories; approval limits and compensating reviews; period-close/reopen authority; retention and legal-hold schedule.

**Exit evidence:** ordinary corrections require no mutation of issued originals; a closed period remains reproducible; every uploaded payment file has an outcome; exceptions cannot silently disappear; approvals identify a person, reason and exact financial action.

### Phase 3 — Enforce agreements and improve collections

**Priority P1 for billing models actually used; otherwise P2.** Add effective-dated rate resolution, staff time approval, recurring/fixed/progress plans and complete prebill review. Link signed engagements. Introduce a collections worklist and then hosted payments/authorized plans, with settlement reconciliation. The existing quote and recurring fields are the starting data, not evidence that billing automation already exists. [Quotes:78](../work/quotes.md#L78), [Customers:160](../work/customers.md#L160).

**Decisions:** actual required fee models; rate precedence; scope/excess rules; approved engagement/payment provider; reminder cadence; family/payer billing needs. Provider costs and implementation choice require a separate selection, not a recommendation to purchase all reference products.

**Exit evidence:** one synthetic engagement follows acceptance → time/work → review → correct fee → issue → payment → deposit → accounting export → correction/refund where applicable. Duplicate retries and returned payments have explicit outcomes. Recurring fees cannot be skipped unnoticed or generated twice.

### Phase 4 — Add decision-quality reporting and selective advantages

**Priority P2/P3.** Build DSO, separate billing/collection realization, capacity utilization and client/job/staff/entity profitability from the reconciled events and historical rates. Add the exception inbox and close package before speculative automation. Earlier metric labeling fixes should not wait for this phase.

**Decisions:** management metric definitions, capacity denominator, cost allocation, seasonal comparison periods and alert thresholds.

**Exit evidence:** every displayed number identifies its basis and cutoff, drills into supporting events, and reconciles to the approved close. An owner can answer “what did we earn, bill, collect, still hold and still need to bill?” separately for each business without interpreting technical ledger markers.
